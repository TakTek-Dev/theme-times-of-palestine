Nabulsi soap, explained: olive oil, time and a city’s craft

How the soap is made, why it cures for weeks, and where it is sold: the questions readers ask most, answered in order.

01What is Nabulsi soap?

A hard soap made in Nablus from olive oil, water and an alkali. It is the colour of ivory, has no added colour or scent, and every bar carries the stamp of the factory that made it.

Soap has been made in the city for centuries, and the bars have been sold across the region for almost as long. [The earliest record, with its source, to be confirmed by the reporter.]

02What goes into it?

Three things: olive oil, water and an alkali, the ingredient that turns oil into soap. The oil has traditionally come from the groves around the city. The alkali was once made from plant ash; today most makers buy it ready-made. [Check the current recipe with the factories we visit.]

Context

Nablus sits among some of the oldest olive groves in the region. Read our feature on the harvest.

03How is it made?

The method has changed little, and most of it is still done by hand.

  1. Cooking

    Oil, water and the alkali are heated together in large vats and stirred until the mixture thickens.

  2. Spreading

    The hot soap is poured onto the factory floor and smoothed into an even layer to set.

  3. Cutting and stamping

    Once firm, the layer is marked into a grid and cut into bars by hand, and each bar is stamped with the factory’s seal.

  4. Curing

    The bars are stacked in tall, hollow towers so that air passes between them, and left to dry.

04Why does it take so long?

Curing lets the water in the soap evaporate, so the bar hardens and lasts longer in use. Makers leave the towers to dry for weeks, and sometimes for months. [Curing times, from the factories we visit.]

06Where is it sold?

In Nablus itself, in markets across the West Bank and in Jerusalem, and abroad, where it is sold as a traditional olive-oil soap. [Where the factories we visit sell their soap today.]
